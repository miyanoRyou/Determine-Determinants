How the Leibniz formula works
  1. Choose one entry from every row so that no two chosen entries share a column. Each such choice is a permutation: the list of column numbers, one per row.
  2. Multiply the chosen entries. That product is the permutation's term, apart from its sign.
  3. Count the inversions: pairs of rows where the earlier row picked a later column. An even count gives a plus sign, an odd count a minus sign.
  4. Add up the signed terms for all n! permutations. The sum is the determinant.

Why this method gets slow

There are n! permutations, so the work grows faster than any power of n. Gaussian elimination needs roughly n³ operations, which is why this app uses elimination only as a cross-check.
